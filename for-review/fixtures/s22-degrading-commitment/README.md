# s22-degrading-commitment (calibration fixture, C65)

A pair of first-hand discovery calls on one new account, taken in together and graded once over the pair. Between the two calls, the technical partner the Consultant works with holds a technical call with the client's systems administrator that the Consultant does not attend and that has no transcript. The pair plants three provenance defects, and the key asserts the repair of each. Fictional client: a commercial laundry and linen rental service with two plants, serving hotels, restaurants and clinics. Everything in it is invented.

## Conventions and deviations worth knowing

- The key uses two of the calibration statuses, `met-with-residual` and `not-obtainable`, and the per-criterion `asked` flag. It does not exercise `advisory`, because both call types resolve cleanly; no calibration fixture carries a positive `advisory` row, because every fixture's type resolves; the value's positive case is the constructed unresolvable fragment of BUILD-PLAN.md section 4.3, item 8 (H50).
- `call_type` is `discovery` for the pair and both `intake.files[].call_type` and both `intake.type_header` labels are `discovery`; the type-header values are the tokens `shared/record-map.md` pins (the ARCHITECTURE.md 8.3 step 5 values in their hyphenated form, with the reasoning in a `_note` beside each field) and the labels are the thirteen of 7.1, verbatim.
- Every list entry and every second-level key is printed on one line, as at stage 1, when the key sat under a 400-line budget. The `readiness` block added in the fix pass keeps the same layout, with its `structure`, `existing` and `verdict` sub-objects opened out one level so their lists read one entry per line; the key is now past the old budget, which the fix pass accepts. The field set and order are unchanged from s1 to s10; `intake` follows as the 26th field and `readiness` as the 27th. The type headers' `direction_of_show` is `direction` since the fix pass.
- Tool references are category words throughout, at most one category per sentence, in the transcripts, the key and this file alike. The monday account is named as itself. No product or vendor is named.
- Dates are relative and fit November 2026: call 1 is a weekday between Monday 2 and Friday 6 November ("It's November", call 2 booked for "Thursday the twelfth" next week, the route supervisor "off today, back Monday"); the technical call is Monday 9 November; call 2 is Thursday 12 November at 2:00 pm Eastern; the walkthrough is Tuesday 24 November at 2:00 pm Eastern, "before the holiday"; the boiler week is the second week of December; the new contract starts the first week of January 2027. No transcript states its own date.
- Person and organisation names were checked against every other fixture folder, the authoring scratch folders and the design documents and collide with none; the consultant appears only as `Consultant`; our colleague is named by role in the key and by first name only in the client's speech.
- The bare word "twenty" appears in call 1 only as minutes per messy par change and as the pack floor's headcount ("twenty-two" and "twenty-four" are the driver counts), never as a shortage count, and the operations director never states a shortage count on either call; the check script asserts both.
- Both files are block exports with start times only, no colliding stamps and no adjacent same-label pairs. Call 1 has one lowercase-initial turn, the tail of the re-joined sentence at 00:25:26; call 2 has none. Tags: [laughs] in both, one [crosstalk] in each, one [inaudible] in call 1, one [muffled] in call 2; " -- " marks restarts and cut-offs in both.
- The authoring scratch (`turns1.py`, `turns2.py`, `build.py`, `check.py`) lives in the session scratchpad and not in `fixtures-work/`; the fixture files are the deliverable and nothing re-runs over them.
