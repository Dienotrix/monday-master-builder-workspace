## How to read the table

- A test is named as it is in the file, with `[A]`, `[W]`, `[G]`, `[F]` or `[P]` for the file. I opened every test I cite. Where a test covers a smaller set than the clause's Test line names, the row says so, and the row is `not met` if the gap is material.
- `met (test)`: a named test asserts the clause's point as its Test line, amended by any decision, asks. `met (inspection)`: an inspection clause, met at a named place. `met (decision)`: a clause the user settled by a decision that says it is met (L218, R65). `changed by decision`: a decision (named in the row: an R, X, Y, Z or U entry, or A30) changed or dropped the clause, and the row says what now holds and which test proves it, if any. `owed to stage 4`: needs WP14's seed records and leak grader (the twenty-five keys' arrays, the three `index` moments of WP8 and WP12, `terms` at release by WP16 and the like), as the clause's Accept line or X20 and X47 say. `not met`: anything else, stated plainly with what is missing.
- A decision that only adds a case to a clause, or widens what it blocks, does not change the clause: the row stays `met (test)` and names the decision and the test.
- A row can end with `Still owed:` naming a later moment (the macOS run, the Windows run, stage 4). That does not change its status; the summary lists them.
- The 25 fixture categories and their planted cases (L214 to L261) are graded by `[G] test_every_case`, which runs the 248 manifest cases as sub-tests (124 planted, 124 twins after R64), and by the fixture self-test `[F]`; a row names the manifest case ids it rests on, as the manifest and README give them.
