# call-intake and build-readiness skills: notes from demo 3

## Result

- Record slug: `industrial-supplies-toledo`, under the home's `clients/`, strand `001-sales-pipeline`, kind `platform`, product CRM.
- Clearance: `cleared to build` (`cleared_for: build`). The one condition besides the approval card: the CRM workspace the user named is not yet in the record, because no account is bound.
- Counts: 8 goals; 15 pain lines in 7 themes (P1 to P7); 8 entities; 13 assumptions (7 gap, 1 call-type, 2 automation-condition, 1 board-architecture, 2 leaning); 7 open questions in the call summary and 5 questions for the next contact in `readiness.md`.
- The transcript is saved with `--fixture`, so the open transcript-keeping question in `CLIENT.md` stays `unanswered`. No exclusion was added: the client asked for nothing to be kept out.
- Both `record.py check` runs at the end of each skill were clean. The leak lint over the client-safe block was clean.

## Notes

1. **The input does not name the client.** call-intake, "Before anything": `record.py init --name "<client name>"`, with a slug that is "never renamed". The transcript names the client's customers, a competitor, its IT provider and a product line, but never the company itself, and it has no header. The skill gives best-guess rules for the call type, a speaker's role and a package's order, and none for the client's name. Because I am a subagent and the brief forbids improvising past a script, but this was not a script refusal, I took a descriptive placeholder name and slug from what the client says it is (an industrial supplies distributor and where its main site is), and listed the name as a `gap` assumption in `readiness.md`. The skill should say what to do: either ask the user before `init` (the slug is permanent, so it is worth one question), or take a descriptive placeholder, mark it assumed, and point at `client rename`, which changes the name but never the slug. If the placeholder is the rule, the slug rule should say how to build a placeholder slug that will still read well once the real name is known.

2. **The line budget of the call summary does not fit a full discovery call.** call-intake, "Writing the summaries", and the record map's 120-line budget for `calls/<stem>.md`. A forty-minute call with three client speakers gave a first draft of 148 lines in the reference's shape: one line per quote, figure, tool, question and commitment. `record.py check` warned at 121. I dropped the blank line after each heading and merged lines (figures by theme, three commitments on one line, tools by category, two open questions together), which breaks the shape's "one line per item". The skill should either raise the budget for a call summary (for example by call length or by client speaker count), or say which sections may be condensed and how, so that the readiness check still reads them heading by heading.

3. **The time grammar of the file and the checker disagree.** call-intake, "Reading a call": "Locations follow it: `@HH:MM:SS` where lines carry times". This file's lines carry `[MM:SS]`. `record.py check` recognises only a location of the form `@H:MM:SS` or `@HH:MM:SS`, so `@03:44` would raise "a quote marker with no location". I wrote every location as `@00:MM:SS` and said so in the summary's call-type section. The skill should say that minute-and-second stamps are written with a zero hour, so the checker and a reader agree.

4. **The client-side agreement was our side's playback.** call-intake, "Always": what the user's side "recaps or plays back is recorded as the user's side's". At the end of the call our side played back five scope items and each client speaker said yes; the sales leader then closed it in his own words. The shape has no place for "our side's wording, confirmed by the client". I kept the playback as our side's, recorded the client's closing sentence as goal G8, and in `readiness.md` treated the stages our side named in that playback as an assumption. The skill should say how a confirmed playback is recorded (for example a `## Agreed` line: our side's wording, the client's confirming quotes and locations), and whether a "yes" to a playback counts as the client's goal.

5. **No section for hard constraints.** call-intake shapes. The client gave three non-negotiables: margin and contract pricing visible only to named roles, the business system untouched, and visit logging under about a minute on a phone. None is a goal, a pain or a figure. I put the margin rule under goals (G7), the system rule under current tools, and the one-minute rule under pains (P7) and figures; build-readiness then carried the margin rule as an unmet need because the permissions are not probed. The shape should have a `## Constraints` heading (the rule, the quote, the location, the speaker) that build-readiness reads into structure and unmet needs.

6. **The quarantine rule is too blunt for our side's what-if questions.** call-intake, "Reading a call", "Quarantine": words "under a hypothetical" are marked `[X]` and are never fact. Here our side asked "say nothing changes" and "if they had account records ... what would you say?", and the client answered with its own estimates (a ramp of six months against nine today). Those are the client's opinions, not seed data in a demonstration. I marked only the ramp estimate `[X]` in figures and did not quarantine the cost figures the client worked out in answer to direct questions. The skill should separate a demonstration's seed data (never fact) from a client's estimate given under our side's what-if (the client's opinion, usable as a figure with that label).

7. **The build target is set at `init` without a word in the skill.** call-intake, "Before anything", and build-readiness, part 4 ("The build target from `CLIENT.md`, with its reason"). `init` wrote `account_kind: own` and `build_target: own-account` by default; the skill does not say so or ask. On the call our side offered "a first version built in your own account", meaning the client's account. The record's default happened to match this demo (the user's own CRM test workspace), so I recorded the call's offer under "What the input cannot see" and in the build target's reason. Also, `CLIENT.md` holds no reason for the build target, which the readiness guide asks to copy "from CLIENT.md". call-intake should say that `init` defaults to own-account and that a call naming another target is a question for the user; `CLIENT.md` or the strand should carry the reason.

8. **Writing `STRAND.md` changes `CLIENT.md`.** build-readiness, "Writing and checking", step 3: set `STRAND.md`, then `products_in_scope` in `CLIENT.md`. I read both shas first and wrote `STRAND.md`; the guard then refused `CLIENT.md` because the strand-index update had changed it. The refusal said to re-read, which fixed it. The step should say to read the `CLIENT.md` sha only after the `STRAND.md` write.

9. **The parent-row-with-roll-up pattern reads oddly on native CRM boards.** build-readiness, part 4, and the guide's `### Patterns` line ("<children> as child rows"). Opportunities and activities move independently of the account, so the pattern applies, but on the native CRM boards they are rows of their own boards connected to the account, not child rows. I wrote the pattern as connected rows with the roll-up on the account. The skill should say how the pattern is written when the children live on native boards.

10. **The call date is not in the file.** call-intake, "Saving the inputs", step 4: `--date` is "the date the input shows or the user gives, else today". The call says only that it is the second week of September. I saved with today's date, so the stem carries the intake date, and wrote `call_date: "unknown"` with the spoken clue in the call-type section. The skill should say that the stem's date is then the save date, not the call date, or let a spoken date with a stated basis be used.

11. **The exclusions template is not what the skill describes.** call-intake, "Writing the summaries", step 4, and build-readiness, "Writing and checking", step 1: "The template's table is not read by the lint, so put the heading after the table". The template `init` wrote has no table: it already opens with `## Exclusions` and a comment showing the row form. No exclusion was needed here, so nothing broke. Both skills should drop the sentence about the table.

12. **Smaller points.**
    - call-intake gives no signal for the `seat` field. I wrote `partner-led proposed`, since our side is an outside consultant.
    - The record map makes call-intake the owner of the "open commitments" block in `STRAND.md`, but the skill never says to write it and the template has no such heading. I left the commitments in the summary only.
    - The summary shape asks for the speaker label "as the file writes it". Here our side's label is a role word, and the skill does not say that our side's label is taken to be the user. I recorded it as the user, observed.
    - Every record write also updated the home's `clients-index.json` (digests only) through `record.py`'s automatic index step. Neither skill mentions that intake writes outside the client folder.
    - build-readiness's conditions list "a probe not yet run for a kind the build plans". The quiet-quote follow-up is an automation, and the probe list covers only agents, Vibe apps and workflows, so I listed no probe condition. The skill could say whether automations need one.

No other refusal. One extra command, not a skill step: a bare `leak_lint.py scan --strand 001` with no path, run as a final sweep, returned `could-not-run` (exit 3) because there was no draft or hand-off file to check. Nothing needed it, and nothing was changed after it.
