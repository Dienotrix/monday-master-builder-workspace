# s13-header-absent (calibration fixture, C57b)

The header-absent twin of s12. One discovery call with a translation company, exported as a block transcript with no participant block, no title, no identifiers and no call date. Three things are planted in front of the parser and the key asserts the repair of each. Fictional client: Corvane Language Services, a thirty-four-person translation company in Pittsburgh. Everything in it is invented.

## Conventions and deviations worth knowing

- The key uses two of the calibration statuses, `met-with-residual` and `not-obtainable`, and the per-criterion `asked` flag. It does not exercise `advisory`, because the call type resolves cleanly; no calibration fixture carries a positive `advisory` row, because every fixture's type resolves; the value's positive case is the constructed unresolvable fragment of BUILD-PLAN.md section 4.3, item 8 (H50).
- The three silent attendees' surnames are never spoken, and the key records all three by first name and role with the surname unknown: "Torsten (finance manager; surname unknown)", the same as Leonor and Harriet. The preamble (line 2) names the finance manager by first name only and the invite itself is not an input, so the key cannot carry a surname for him; the user holds the invite, so the gap is a question to the user (pre-build question 12), never a guess. The accepted behaviours say that reading is the correct one and that taking a surname from anywhere but the input, or inventing one for anybody, is wrong.
- The narrator (the operations director) is the loudest speaker by words; the speaker introduced in the second roster turn (the senior PM) is the second-loudest and carries most of the pain evidence. C3's rationale mentions losing "the call's loudest speaker" with a dropped second turn; the fixture row does not require that, and this fixture plants the weaker form on purpose so the loudest speaker is not also the narrator of the roster.
- Zero adjacent same-label pairs across 239 pairs, which is consistent with an exporter that merges consecutive same-speaker paragraphs; the key asserts it is a flag needing a second signal and that the file sits in the no-action band.
- Every list entry (a rubric row, a pain, an objection, an intake entry, a readiness entry) is printed on one line. The stage-1 400-line budget held until the fix pass added the `readiness` block in the same style; the key is 511 lines now. The field set and order are unchanged from s1 to s10, with `intake` and then `readiness` after them.
- The `readiness` block answers the v1 build question by the fix-pass brief's definitions; the rubric, `expected_verdict` and `numbers` answer the v2 sales question and were not edited. The accounting system's state in the block and in the key is "integration and write behaviour unresolved, pending the finance manager", with the first version staying clear of it as our own safe default, labelled as ours; the one status is "a shared status vocabulary; confirm the exact labels".
- Call type and the type header use the tokens `shared/record-map.md` pins for the type header (the ARCHITECTURE.md 8.3 step 5 values in their hyphenated form, with the fixture's reasoning in a `_note` beside each field) and the labels of 7.1 verbatim.
- Tool references are category words throughout, one category per sentence: the translation tool, a spreadsheet, the accounting system, a chat channel, the shared drive, the client's portal, email.
- Dates are relative and fit November 2026: a Tuesday call on or about 10 November, the walkthrough Thursday 19 November at 10:00 Eastern, the framework contract from January. No transcript states its own date; the preamble's "Tuesday" is the user's note.
- Real places (Pittsburgh, Cleveland) are named on purpose, as some of s1 to s10 do (s3 and s4 name cities); no real organisation, product or brand appears.
