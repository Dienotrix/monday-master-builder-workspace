# The build-readiness check — specification

Written 2026-10-13 as the planner's input to stage 1b, and folded the same day into SPEC (the build-readiness check) and ARCHITECTURE section 3.3; re-cut 2026-10-14 under A24 to A28, which open the check with goals and pains (A27) and let user-approved assumptions carry a proof of concept (A25), with the stage-2 consistency check of that day applied; emails as pasted text or screenshots, and the goal-quote grader check, per the user's answers of 2026-10-14. The folded text in SPEC and ARCHITECTURE follows this file. Under A19 this check replaces sales grading in v1: the twelve criteria, the gates, the verdicts and the gap questions in sales terms move to v2 and are not read by any v1 skill. Everything below is written for "the user", works for whoever uses the plugin, and names no client, person or account.

## 1. The one question it answers

Do we know enough to build a proof of concept worth showing, and if not, what exactly would close the gap? It never asks whether the client is ready to buy.

It runs on the synthesised record after intake of whatever arrived: one call, a package of calls, client documents, an email as pasted text or a screenshot, or any mix in any order (A15, A16, interview Q7). It reads the live map and earlier strands when the client already has a build (A14), the bound account when one is bound (products, tier, native boards, existing workspaces), the client's exclusion list (Q14) and the research brief when one exists. Thin input never blocks it. Thin input widens the assumptions list and makes the first build more generic (Q7, clarification), because a basic demo beats nothing: a generic workspace with its assumptions listed is built and shown rather than a wait for better inputs (A27). Assumptions the user approves may carry a proof of concept as long as they stay visibly unconfirmed; a working solution needs its structural assumptions confirmed or explicitly accepted (A25).

## 2. What it produces

One block, `readiness`, kept in the strand as `readiness.md` and mirrored in each fixture's answer key under the same name. Nine parts, in this order.

1. **Goals and pains.** First the client's stated goals, each in their own words with its verbatim quote and location and the role of who said it, so the user sees what the client wants before what hurts (A27). Then each pain with its verbatim quote and location, who lives it, whether it was stated or excavated, and the wanted outcome in the client's own words. Beside them, the **unconfirmed client signals**: a client tie that our side never confirmed is carried forward so that the next question is pointed, and it is never counted as evidence (the standing line from the fixture review: inference targets questions; it never fills evidence).
2. **Entities and how they move.** The things the client tracks, and for each: what it belongs to; whether it moves, expires, is owned or is scheduled independently of its parent; whether it is a shared resource booked across jobs; whether it is an artefact that goes stale when its inputs change; and the source of each statement, marked spoken, observed, documented or assumed. This part is where the first standing question is answered in writing: **what moves independently of what?**
3. **Product and structure.** Which product or products (Work Management, CRM, Service; the portfolio default on Enterprise Work Management, A6), one workspace per client and per product (Q8, Q9), the native boards reused (A5), and the proposed shape. Where the evidence does not settle the shape, the shape is recorded as an **open structural question with a leaning**: the option, the quotes it rests on, and the live condition that would settle it, the same shape a fork takes (s24). An open structural question does not by itself stop a proof of concept: the build rests on its leaning with the user's yes and marks it assumed, the card says what a later switch to the other option would and would not keep, and the user's blocking flag on a fork stays the one way to stop that part (A25). Where children move independently of a parent, the blueprint applies the **standard parent-row-with-roll-up pattern** rather than one row carrying one value for many things. Then the second standing question is answered for every pain: **which element fixes this pain, and does the shape fix the pain or copy today's tool?** A pain with no element aimed at it is a finding, not an omission to pass over.
4. **What already exists.** In the bound account: the live map, earlier strands, the client's own prototypes, the native boards found by lineage and id. In the client's current tools: the systems that stay system of record, field by field, so the blueprint derives its read-only columns and mirrors from an authority table rather than from our own demo narration.
5. **Assumptions.** Every gap the first build fills by assumption, labelled as an assumption in the record, each with the question that would replace it. Beside the gaps, the other things the build assumes: the call type when intake could only guess it (A27); the automation conditions and the general overview of the board architecture when the client cannot be reached before a proof of concept, checked with the client at the next contact; and each fork or open structural question built on its leaning (A25). Column names and status labels are not assumptions to confirm one by one; they show on the approval card for the user to check. Assumptions shape the build and the questions. They live in the record, on the approval card and in the build notes (A23), where each open item gets a short paragraph: what was assumed, why, and what the client needs to clarify. They never become evidence, and they are never written into monday as text, let alone as if the client had said them. The user is the one who confirms or accepts an assumption, passing on what the client said; the plugin never deals with the client.
6. **Needs the build does not meet.** Anything the client needs that v1 cannot build or has not probed, named as a need with its clock where the client gave one. A product limit never deletes a client need from the record (s15). This part is also where a bespoke, non-platform ask is recorded and handed off (A13).
7. **AI opportunities.** Suggested AI elements, each tied to one pain or stated need and to one element kind: a monday Workflow, an agent with an authored prompt, an AI column, a Vibe app, a sequence (A17, A18). Each carries its round: round one when the client asked for AI or workflows, round two otherwise (Q5, Q16). Each names the probe finding it rests on, or says that the kind is unprobed. The plugin never states a credit figure, price or package (A17).
8. **Clearance, as what the user is cleared to do.** One of three values, never a bare "met" (the record keeps it in the pinned field `verdict`, see section 7):
   - `cleared to build`: at least one pain with an entity and a wanted outcome; the product chosen; the structure stated, with its leanings; the build target named (which account, which workspaces).
   - `cleared to build, generic`: the same, but the structure rests mostly on assumptions; the build goes ahead as a generalised version, built and shown with its assumptions listed rather than held for better inputs (A27), and the assumptions list is its agenda for the next contact.
   - `not yet`: no pain or entity firm enough to build on; the questions in part 9 are the next step.
   Beside the clearance, every outstanding condition that is not the check's own business: the approval stop still to pass, an A11 decision that first matters now, a workspace not yet named, a probe not yet run. The clearance is a statement about this record today; it is re-run after every new input.
9. **Questions for the next contact.** At most five, ordered, each routed to the role that can answer it, for the user to ask; the plugin never deals with the client. A question is a **read-back** of the client's own words where an unconfirmed signal exists, and a **discovery question** where none does. A cross-call or document-versus-call conflict becomes a question with both statements shown, never a resolution. A fork or an open structural question carries its leaning, which the build rests on with the user's yes, and its settling condition (A25). Each question shows what is already known, so the user never asks for something the record already holds. These questions are for the next contact, not a stop before the build: before the approval card the plugin asks the user only what it cannot infer, and everything else is assumed, labelled and shown on the card (A27).

## 3. Rules the check inherits unchanged

- Evidence rules from intake: verbatim quotes at their locations, the four confidence states, the citation tiers for documents and second-hand material, the refusal tier.
- The non-evidence rule: our own framing, recaps, playbacks and demo stage lists never become the client's requirement. The call type header still tells the check who was showing what, which is how our-side framing is told apart from the client's words. The type is intake's best guess, marked assumed, and a correction by the user re-runs this check (A27). The type's scope matrix for criteria is v2 and is not read.
- Both sides of a conflict are kept with their sources; the most recent statement does not win across speakers or calls (A15).
- Neutral naming, the leak lint and the three open A11 decisions apply as everywhere else.

## 4. What it retires from v1

The per-pain checks, R1 to R12, `met`, `partial`, `missing`, `advisory`, `met-with-residual`, `not-obtainable`, the five gates and the three verdicts, the bleed and upside numbers, and the gap questions in sales terms. The files that hold them are kept for v2 and no v1 skill reads them. The answer keys keep their grading blocks for v2; v1 graders read only the `intake` block, the pains, the products, the blueprint, the forks and the new `readiness` block.

## 5. Where it sits in the first-hour path, and in the usual one to three days

Intake, then this check, then the blueprint, then one approval stop, then the build with read-back on every write. The check runs from the record in minutes and its output is the first section of the approval card, "what this build rests on", so the user approves the assumptions along with the structure. On a proof of concept in the user's own account, where notifications reach only the user, that approval is one yes for the whole revision, lists and irreversible steps included; in a client's account, and for any list whose rows notify real people, each list keeps its own yes (A28). Round one builds the workspace with sample rows drawn from the client's real world minus the exclusion list, for realism over the hour: dozens per board where dashboards and roll-ups need real numbers, a handful where a board only has to read well, plausible invented names and figures in the client's industry from the call and the research brief, and every value marked as sample in the record so none is ever cited as a fact (A28). Round two builds the AI layer, unless the client asked for AI first.

The hour is the floor, not the typical case (A27). There are normally one to three days of preparation before the client meeting; in that time the deep research runs before the first build, the sample data is refined, and round two is built and audited before the first showing. The two rounds are an order of work, not a wait. The plugin proposes the cadence from the time available, and the user sets it.

## 6. What the fixtures must carry for it

Each of the 25 answer keys gained a `readiness` block in the fixture fix pass (complete 2026-10-13), derived from what the key already held: its pains, products, blueprint notes, forks and open questions, recut into the nine parts above. Two grader checks come straight from the fixture review: every parent-and-child relation in a key carries an independence note, and every pain names the element that fixes it or says plainly that none does. The sweep list at the end of `for-review/FEEDBACK.md` was the checklist the fix pass applied while adding the block. The keys received the `goals` list of part 1 in the goals pass of 2026-10-14, with the record-map pin of section 7 (A27): 85 goals across 24 keys, s10 stating none, every goal quote verified at its location. A third grader check joined the two then, so the grader checks are three: every goal in the `goals` list carries its verbatim quote at its location.

## 7. Shape, pinned in the record map

`readiness/1`: `goals[]` (id, goal in the client's words, quote, location, speaker role, pains: the ids of the pains it relates to), `pains[]` (id, quote, location, speaker role, stated or excavated, outcome), `signals[]` (quote, location, pain, what was not confirmed), `entities[]` (name, parent, moves independently: yes, no or unknown, shared resource, goes stale, source), `structure` (products, workspaces, native reuse, leanings[] with option, quotes and condition, patterns applied, element per pain), `existing` (account objects, incumbents with field authority), `assumptions[]` (statement, question, kind: gap, call type, automation condition, board architecture or leaning; state: assumed or accepted, a confirmed one becoming evidence and leaving the list), `unmet_needs[]` (need, clock, why not met), `ai_opportunities[]` (pain or need, kind, round, probe basis), `verdict` (cleared_for: build, build-generic or not-yet, so that the check's own `not yet` is never confused with the `none` a fresh `readiness.md` carries before the check has run; conditions[]), `questions[]` (text, kind: read-back or discovery, role, what is known). The fixture fix pass of 2026-10-13 pinned every field except `goals[]` and the two new `assumptions[]` fields in `shared/record-map.md` ("Pinned: readiness"), with their final snake_case names; `goals[]`, as `id`, `goal`, `quote`, `location`, `speaker_role` and `pains[]`, and the two `assumptions[]` fields were pinned there in the goals pass of 2026-10-14, which also moved `cleared_for`'s pinned `none` to `not-yet` in the record map and in the one answer key that carried it (s10). The assumption kinds are spelled `gap`, `call-type`, `automation-condition`, `board-architecture` and `leaning`.

## 8. Decided by the user (2026-10-13 and 2026-10-14)

- Five stays the ceiling for questions after a thin input; a generic build carries more assumptions, not more questions.
- `cleared to build, generic` needs no separate yes: the approval card's "what this build rests on" section, which lists the assumptions, is the user's yes to them. On a proof of concept in the user's own account that same yes covers the whole revision (A28).
- The check opens with goals and pains (A27).
- The call type is a best guess, marked assumed; intake stops to ask only when it cannot tell (A27).
- User-approved assumptions may carry a proof of concept while they stay visibly unconfirmed, and an open structural question is built on its leaning with the user's yes. Recording `signed-off` warns, listing every structural assumption not yet confirmed or accepted, and does not block (A25, answered 2026-10-14).

## 9. Reconciliation after the document maps (2026-10-13)

- **"Cleared to show" belongs to the audit, not to this check.** Nothing can be cleared for showing before a build exists, is audited and is read back, so the audit report ends with `cleared to show` or `not cleared to show`, and this check keeps its three values. The record marks a version `shown` as an event the user records, in place of the old `demoed` state.
- **Build target and workspaces (A21).** Part 3 names the build target recorded at setup, the user's own account by default, and plans brand-new workspaces for the client, one per product, unless the build continues workspaces already built for the same client. The plugin writes into no other workspace, and an account-level object such as an AI agent may read and write only that client's workspaces.
- **The thin-input ceiling stays at five questions** until the user says otherwise (section 8, first item); a generic build carries its assumptions in the record, not in extra questions.
- **Home and shape.** The check is its own skill, `build-readiness`, with one reference file and the `readiness.md` strand file; the shape `readiness/1` and the three clearance strings were pinned in `shared/record-map.md` in the fixture fix pass, where `discovery.md` and its grading values are fenced off as v2, and `goals[]` joined the pin in the goals pass of 2026-10-14 (A27).
